\section{Problem}
\label{s:problem}

We are given a set of candidate facility locations $F$ and a set of customers
$C$. Each facility $f \in F$ has an opening cost $o_f$ and a capacity $q_f$.
Each customer $c \in C$ has demand $d_c$ and a service (allocation) cost
$a_{c,f}$ for each potential facility $f$. Vehicles are identical: each pays
a fixed usage cost $u$ and may drive at most $T$ total round-trip distance,
where $\delta_{c,f}$ is the round-trip distance between $c$ and $f$. Up to
$V = |C|$ vehicles may be deployed at any facility. The objective is the sum
of opening, service, and vehicle-usage costs.

The underlying problem is discrete (e.g.\ a facility is open or not, a
vehicle exists or it does not), so the cost-minimising plan is the optimum
of an integer program (IP). Because the IP is NP-hard but its LP relaxation
is solvable in polynomial time, and because every LP-feasible solution
provides a valid lower bound on the IP optimum, we report the ceiling of
the LP optimum as a fast lower bound on the true business cost.

\section{Constraint Model}
\label{s:model}

We adopt the natural mixed-integer formulation of capacitated facility
location with vehicle workload, and then \emph{relax all integrality}: every
decision variable becomes continuous on a real interval. The resulting LP
is what \texttt{src/lpinstance.py} hands to OR-Tools.

We use an \emph{aggregated} form: $v_f$ counts vehicles at facility $f$ as
a single continuous quantity, rather than indexing each vehicle slot
separately. This keeps the model compact (one variable per facility for
vehicles, one per customer-facility pair for assignment) at the cost of
some LP tightness: distance is constrained in aggregate, not per truck.
The trade-off is acceptable because the LP only needs to certify a lower
bound, not implement a plan.

\section{Decision Variables}
\label{s:vars}

All variables are continuous (no integrality). Let $C$ and $F$ denote the
customer and facility index sets.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $y_f \in [0,1]$, one per facility $f \in F$: the fraction to which
        facility $f$ is opened. In the IP this is binary; in the LP
        relaxation it can be any real value in $[0,1]$.
  \item $x_{c,f} \in [0,1]$, one per pair $(c, f) \in C \times F$: the
        fraction of customer $c$'s demand that is served from facility $f$.
        In the IP this is binary (each customer is assigned to exactly one
        facility); the LP allows split assignments.
  \item $v_f \in [0, V]$, one per facility $f \in F$: the continuous number
        of vehicles deployed at facility $f$. In the IP this is integer.
\end{itemize}

\section{Constraints}
\label{s:constraints}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Full service.} Each customer is fully served across some
        combination of facilities:
        \[
          \sum_{f \in F} x_{c,f} \;=\; 1
          \qquad \forall c \in C.
        \]
  \item \textbf{Open before serve.} A customer may only draw service from a
        facility to the extent that facility is open:
        \[
          x_{c,f} \;\le\; y_f
          \qquad \forall c \in C,\; f \in F.
        \]
        This is the disaggregated (strong) form; in the IP it forces $x = 1
        \Rightarrow y = 1$.
  \item \textbf{Facility capacity.} The demand served from $f$ may not
        exceed $f$'s capacity, scaled by its openness:
        \[
          \sum_{c \in C} d_c\, x_{c,f} \;\le\; q_f\, y_f
          \qquad \forall f \in F.
        \]
        Scaling by $y_f$ is what makes the LP relaxation honest: a
        half-open facility carries only half the capacity.
  \item \textbf{Vehicle workload (driving limit).} Total round-trip
        distance dispatched from $f$ cannot exceed the workload that
        $v_f$ vehicles can absorb:
        \[
          \sum_{c \in C} \delta_{c,f}\, x_{c,f} \;\le\; T\, v_f
          \qquad \forall f \in F.
        \]
        This is the LP surrogate for the per-vehicle limit. With $v_f$
        continuous the LP can split distance across fractional vehicles,
        which is the main source of slack between LP and IP.
  \item \textbf{Vehicles require an open facility.} A closed facility
        cannot deploy vehicles, and we cannot deploy more than $V$:
        \[
          v_f \;\le\; V \cdot y_f
          \qquad \forall f \in F.
        \]
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Objective.} Minimise the sum of opening, service, and
truck-usage costs:
\[
  \min \;
  \sum_{f \in F} o_f\, y_f
  \;+\; \sum_{c \in C}\sum_{f \in F} a_{c,f}\, x_{c,f}
  \;+\; u \sum_{f \in F} v_f .
\]

\section{Implementation}
\label{s:impl}

We use Google OR-Tools'
\texttt{ortools.linear\_solver.python.model\_builder} API with the SCIP
backend constructed by the stencil. Since every variable is declared with
\texttt{model.new\_num\_var} (continuous), SCIP has nothing to branch on
and solves the model as a pure linear program through its LP backend; no
integer programming happens in the submitted code path. We considered
swapping in the dedicated LP backend \texttt{GLOP} (which would make the
``it's an LP'' story unambiguous), but kept SCIP to leave the stencil's
constructor untouched.

The full model lives in \texttt{src/lpinstance.py}. The \texttt{solve()}
method declares the variables, posts the five constraint families above,
sets the objective with \texttt{model.minimize}, and calls
\texttt{solver.solve(model)}. The \texttt{Result} field reported to the
grader is $\lceil \text{LP\_objective} \rceil$.

\section{Verification}
\label{s:verify}

LP correctness is checked two ways:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Constraint re-check (\texttt{src/verify.py}).} A standalone
        script independently re-evaluates every modelled constraint on the
        fractional $y, x, v$ returned by SCIP (tolerance $10^{-6}$) and
        recomputes the objective from those variables to confirm it
        matches \texttt{solver.objective\_value}. All eleven input
        instances pass.
  \item \textbf{LP $\le$ IP on toy instances.} For \texttt{toy\_45.scm}
        and \texttt{toy\_65.scm} the IP optima are known by hand
        (45 and 65 respectively, derived from $2$ opening + $3$ service
        + $2 \times 20$ or $3 \times 20$ truck usage). Our LP returns
        $35$ and $38$, both strictly below the IP optimum --- the
        relaxation is sound.
\end{itemize}
